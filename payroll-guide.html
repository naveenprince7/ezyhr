The Step-by-Step Guide to Modern Payroll Processing

A practical walkthrough of how companies structure compensation packages, factor in monthly attendance, calculate statutory withholdings, and disburse error-free salaries.

⚖️
Educational Disclaimer: This guide provides general operational concepts. Statutory tax rules, labor rates, and local withholding laws vary by jurisdiction and require independent verification with a qualified accountant.
1

Step 1: Establishing the Salary Structure (CTC Breakdown)

Before processing a single paycheck, each employee's annual Cost-to-Company (CTC) must be broken down into defined monthly components. In modern compensation planning, this comprises Fixed Earnings, Allowances, and Employer Statutory Contributions.

Annual CTC = Basic Salary + HRA + Special Allowances + Employer PF (12%) + Gratuity + Variable Bonus
Basic Salary (Typically 40–50%)

The foundational component upon which statutory provident fund and gratuity calculations are anchored.

House Rent Allowance (HRA)

Typically 40% to 50% of Basic Pay, often partially tax-exempt upon submitting valid rent receipts.

2

Step 2: Gathering Monthly Attendance & Leave Inputs

Payroll cannot be calculated on theoretical work days. Between the 20th and 25th of the payroll cycle, HR administrators freeze attendance data to identify total payable days.

Payable Days = Total Days in Month - (Unapproved Absences + Unpaid Leaves Without Pay [LWP])

If an employee joined mid-month (e.g. October 15th), their pay is pro-rated according to the exact number of calendar or working days elapsed from their start date.

3

Step 3: Calculating Gross Earnings

Once payable days are determined, calculate the pro-rated gross compensation including any one-time incentives, overtime premiums, or referral bonuses earned in that specific cycle.

Gross Salary = (Monthly Fixed Components × [Payable Days / Total Days]) + Overtime Pay + Bonuses + Arrears
4

Step 4: Computing Statutory & Voluntary Deductions

This is where manual spreadsheets most frequently break. Statutory withholdings must be deducted from Gross Earnings on behalf of government agencies:

  • Employee Provident Fund (PF): Typically 12% of (Basic + DA), matched by an equivalent employer contribution.
  • Employee State Insurance (ESI): Typically 0.75% for eligible wage earners below statutory thresholds.
  • Professional Tax (PT): State-specific monthly labor deduction (typically ₹150 – ₹200).
  • Tax Deducted at Source (TDS / Income Tax): Calculated on projected annual taxable income based on the employee's declared investment regime (Old vs New).
Total Deductions = Employee PF + ESI + Professional Tax + Monthly TDS + Company Loan Recoveries
5

Step 5: Net Salary Disbursement

Net Pay is the final amount transferred to the employee's designated bank account.

Net Pay = Gross Earnings - Total Deductions

HR generates an encrypted direct-debit batch file (in NEFT/RTGS standard banking format) and uploads it to the corporate banking portal for instantaneous salary credit.

6

Step 6: Post-Payroll Reporting & Payslip Delivery

The cycle finishes with distribution and statutory remittance:

1. Payslip Distribution

Encrypted digital PDF payslips are published to employee self-service portals and delivered to registered email addresses.

2. Statutory Remittance

Remit deducted PF, ESI, and TDS taxes to regulatory bodies before mandatory monthly deadlines (e.g., the 7th or 15th).
